Re-ran `capture.sh` two hours after the original capture (02:14–02:20 UTC) and

**No service changed, appeared, or disappeared in two hours.** Every surface that

## The one difference is a Cloudflare nonce, not drift

`begg-ai-command-center` hashed differently but was the **same 4361 bytes**. A
byte-level diff isolates it to 21 bytes inside Cloudflare's challenge-platform
bootstrap:

```
old  window.__CF$cv$params={r:'a42fb9639f4dcdb3',t:'MTc5MDczNDU3Mg=='}
new  window.__CF$cv$params={r:'a430613eda893c79',t:'MTc5MDc0MTQ0Nw=='}
```

`r` is a per-request ray id and `t` a timestamp — both change on every request.
So the capture is deterministic apart from this, exactly as its header claims.

**Practical note for anyone re-running this:** expect a hash mismatch on
`begg-ai-command-center` every time. Compare its size, or strip
`__CF$cv$params` before hashing. Do not report it as a change.

## What this also settles

The command center is **up**: HTTP **401**, 4361 B, Cloudflare-served,
auth-gated — matching the 2026-09-29 23:29 record. This is the second
independent confirmation that the 404 recorded in an earlier revision of
`CURRENT_STATE.md` was a false alarm caused by probing a guessed
`onrender.com` hostname (see the correction in that file).

## Caveat, stated plainly

This proves the *published surfaces* are unchanged. It does not prove the
services behind them are unchanged — a deploy that alters behaviour without
altering the published OpenAPI document would not show up here. The capture is a
preservation record, not a deployment detector.
